1. Robot Architecture & Kinematic Chain

The robot is an agile wheeled biped consisting of a floating base pelvis/torso and two bilateral 3-link articulated legs terminating in independent driven hub wheels. The mechanical CAD assembly (exported from OnShape into CAD/robot.xml and CAD/scene.xml) defines an 8-DOF actuated multibody system resting on ground plane contacts.

2. CAD Asymmetric Assembly Calibration & Nominal Stance

In CAD/robot.xml, the right and left leg subassemblies originate from OnShape with asymmetrical local joint coordinate zero-points. If the joint positions are initialized to zero ($q_i = 0$), the right knee points backward while the left knee points forward, inducing a catastrophic static pitch tilt of $23.4^\circ$ and wheel contact separation.

Nominal Sagittal Stance Alignment

By solving the closed-loop inverse kinematic kinematic loop equations matching the forward sagittal stance height and wheel axle collinearity, the exact calibrated joint offsets were derived:

$$\begin{aligned} q_{h,R,\text{nom}} &= +0.314503\,\text{rad} \quad (+18.02^\circ) \\ q_{k,R,\text{nom}} &= -0.052919\,\text{rad} \quad (-3.03^\circ) \\ q_{h,L,\text{nom}} &= -0.212551\,\text{rad} \quad (-12.18^\circ) \\ q_{k,L,\text{nom}} &= +0.253279\,\text{rad} \quad (+14.51^\circ) \end{aligned}$$

At these nominal angles, the wheel centers are aligned symmetrically at $y = -0.0713\,\text{m}$, the pitch tilt of the torso is exactly $0.000^\circ$ ($\text{Up-Z} = 1.0000$), and the nominal standing height is $z_{\text{nom}} = 0.1172\,\text{m}$ (on the $8\,\text{cm}$ platform) and relative height above wheels is $h_{\text{rel},\text{nom}} = -0.0128\,\text{m}$.

3. Nonlinear Dynamics & acados SQP-RTI Formulation

To enable agile dynamic balancing, variable pendulum length crouching, and ZMP curve banking, the robot dynamics were formalized into an 8-state, 3-control nonlinear optimal control problem (OCP) using CasADi and compiled into C using acados SQP-RTI.

The state and control vectors are:

$$\mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} p \\ v \\ \theta \\ \omega \\ l \\ \dot{l} \\ \psi \\ \dot{\psi} \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^8, \quad \mathbf{u} = \begin{bmatrix} \tau_w \\ F_l \\ \tau_{\text{roll}} \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^3$$

Coupled pitch-longitudinal equations of motion:

$$I_y(l) = I_{y0} + M l^2, \quad M_{\text{tot}} = M + 2m_w + 2\frac{I_w}{R^2}$$ $$\det M(l, \theta) = M_{\text{tot}} I_y(l) - (M l \cos\theta)^2$$ $$\dot{v} = \frac{I_y(l) \left(\frac{\tau_w}{R} + M l \omega^2 \sin\theta - 2M\dot{l}\omega\cos\theta\right) - M l \cos\theta \left(M g l \sin\theta - \tau_w - 2M l \dot{l}\omega\right)}{\det M(l, \theta)}$$ $$\dot{\omega} = \frac{-M l \cos\theta \left(\frac{\tau_w}{R} + M l \omega^2 \sin\theta - 2M\dot{l}\omega\cos\theta\right) + M_{\text{tot}} \left(M g l \sin\theta - \tau_w - 2M l \dot{l}\omega\right)}{\det M(l, \theta)}$$

4. Non-Minimum Phase Dynamics & Drift Elimination

Wheeled inverted pendulums exhibit classic non-minimum phase behavior: because actuation torque $\tau_w$ accelerates both the wheel base and induces an opposing reaction moment on the torso, to accelerate forward the wheels must initially roll backward to establish forward pitch lean:

$$G_{v,\tau}(s) = \frac{V(s)}{\mathcal{T}_w(s)} = \frac{b_1 (s - z_0)(s + z_0)}{s (s - p_0)(s + p_0)}, \quad z_0 = \sqrt{\frac{g}{l}} > 0$$

Standard linear state feedback or decoupled MPC struggles with steady-state tracking error under continuous forward velocity commands. A cascaded pitch feedforward compensator computes the exact dynamic equilibrium tilt:

$$\theta_{\text{ref}} = \operatorname{clamp}\left(K_{p,v} (v - v_{\text{ref}}) + K_{i,v} \int (v - v_{\text{ref}}) dt,\, -\theta_{\text{max}},\, \theta_{\text{max}}\right)$$

This tilts the body forward into the direction of acceleration, eliminating velocity tracking lag and position runaway.

5. Symmetrical Vertical Crouch Control & Height Kinematics

Vertical suspension height $l(q_k)$ is modulated dynamically through coordinated hip-knee flexion. To prevent asymmetric knee motions, the kinematic jacobian $J_{\text{crouch}} = \frac{\partial z}{\partial q}$ governs coordinated joint angles:

$$\begin{aligned} q_{k,R} &= q_{k,R,\text{nom}} - q_{k,\text{current}} \\ q_{k,L} &= q_{k,L,\text{nom}} + q_{k,\text{current}} \\ q_{h,R} &= q_{h,R,\text{nom}} - 0.515 \cdot (q_{k,R} - q_{k,R,\text{nom}}) + 0.40 \cdot \theta_{\text{ref}} \\ q_{h,L} &= q_{h,L,\text{nom}} - 0.705 \cdot (q_{k,L} - q_{k,L,\text{nom}}) - 0.40 \cdot \theta_{\text{ref}} \end{aligned}$$

This preserves pitch equilibrium while altering ride height from $14.0\,\text{cm}$ (crouch) to $17.2\,\text{cm}$ (tall).

6. Interactive Driving & Inward Roll Banking Lean

When cornering at speed $v$ with yaw rate $\dot{\psi}$, lateral centrifugal acceleration $a_c = v \dot{\psi}$ acts on the center of mass. Left uncompensated, this centrifugal moment causes the wheeled biped to tip outward and roll over.

The controller computes the Zero-Moment Point (ZMP) equilibrium lean angle (Ascento Sec III-B.3):

$$\psi_{\text{ref}} = \arctan\left(\frac{v \cdot \dot{\psi}}{g}\right)$$

By actively banking the hip roll joints into the turn ($q_{\text{roll},R} = -\psi_{\text{ref}}, q_{\text{roll},L} = +\psi_{\text{ref}}$), the gravito-inertial net force vector passes directly through the centerline between the two wheel contacts, maintaining equal normal force distribution on both tires during high-speed cornering.

The simulation starts with the robot perched on an elevated platform with an $8\,\text{cm}$ drop ledge:

Platform Drop Specifications:

  • Platform Extents: From $y = -0.80\,\text{m}$ to $y = +0.40\,\text{m}$ (width $1.20\,\text{m}$).
  • Drop Ledge: At $y = +0.40\,\text{m}$, the platform terminates abruptly, dropping $8\,\text{cm}$ to $z = 0.00\,\text{m}$.

The active suspension implements variable virtual impedance control with impact shock dissipation:

$$\tau_{\text{knee}} = -K_{p,\text{susp}} (q_k - q_{k,\text{target}}) - K_{d,\text{susp}}(\dot{q}_k) \dot{q}_k$$ $$K_{d,\text{susp}}(\dot{q}_k) = \begin{cases} 9.0\,\text{N}\cdot\text{s/rad} & \text{if } |\dot{q}_k| > 1.2\,\text{rad/s} \quad \text{(Touchdown impact damping)} \\ 3.5\,\text{N}\cdot\text{s/rad} & \text{otherwise} \quad \text{(Nominal compliance)} \end{cases}$$

8. Hierarchical Whole-Body Quadratic Programming (WBC) Controller

To simultaneously regulate floating-base balancing, dynamic height modulation, roll ZMP lean, and multi-contact constraints across uneven and wavy terrain, a 28-variable Hierarchical Whole-Body Quadratic Program (WBC QP) was developed in controllers/wbc/wbc_controller.py based on the ETH Zurich Ascento framework (Klemm et al., IEEE RAL 2020).

Figure 2: Information flow and mathematical constraint structure of the Hierarchical Whole-Body QP (OSQP) controller.

Mathematical Formulation

The optimization variable vector $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{28}$ stacks:

$$\mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} \ddot{\mathbf{q}} \\ \mathbf{F}_c \\ \boldsymbol{\tau} \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{28}, \quad \ddot{\mathbf{q}} \in \mathbb{R}^{14}, \quad \mathbf{F}_c \in \mathbb{R}^6, \quad \boldsymbol{\tau} \in \mathbb{R}^8$$

subject to the constrained quadratic program:

$$\min_{\mathbf{x}} \frac{1}{2} \mathbf{x}^T P \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{q}_{\text{cost}}^T \mathbf{x} \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \mathbf{l} \le A_{\text{all}} \mathbf{x} \le \mathbf{u}$$

Hard Physical Constraints

  1. Multibody Rigid Dynamics (14 rows): $$M(\mathbf{q}) \ddot{\mathbf{q}} - J_c^T \mathbf{F}_c - S^T \boldsymbol{\tau} = -h(\mathbf{q}, \dot{\mathbf{q}})$$ where $S = [0_{8 \times 6} \quad I_{8 \times 8}]$ selects the 8 actuated joints.
  2. Coulomb Friction Pyramids (8 rows): $$|F_{x,i}| \le \mu F_{z,i}, \quad |F_{y,i}| \le \mu F_{z,i}, \quad i \in \{R, L\}, \quad \mu = 0.85$$
  3. Unilateral Contact Bounds (2 rows): $$F_{z,\text{min}} \le F_{z,i} \le F_{z,\text{max}}, \quad F_{z,\text{min}} = 1.0\,\text{N}, \quad F_{z,\text{max}} = 60.0\,\text{N}$$
  4. Actuator Saturation Bounds (8 rows): $$\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{min}} \le \boldsymbol{\tau} \le \boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{max}}, \quad \boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{max}} = [15, 15, 15, 5, 15, 15, 15, 5]^T\,\text{Nm}$$

Objective Tasks & Resolved Bugs

Critical WBC Implementation Breakthroughs:
  • 3D Rolling Contact Kinematics (Fixing Free-Wheeling): In addition to lateral ($X$) and normal ($Z$) contact constraints, the longitudinal contact direction ($Y$) enforces perfect rolling: $$J_{c,y} \ddot{\mathbf{q}} = -k_d v_{c,y}$$ Because $J_{c,y}$ has $+1.0$ on base velocity and $-R_{\text{wheel}}$ on wheel joint velocity, this couples wheel motor torque directly to forward acceleration ($\ddot{y}_{\text{base}} \approx R_{\text{wheel}} \ddot{q}_{\text{wheel}}$), preventing wheel motors from being zeroed out by torque regularization.
  • Relative Height Task (Fixing Platform & Wave Drop Collapse): Instead of penalizing absolute world height $(z_{\text{base}} - z_{\text{target}})^2$, the height task regulates height relative to the wheel axle midpoint: $$h_{\text{rel}} = z_{\text{base}} - \frac{z_{\text{wheel},R} + z_{\text{wheel},L}}{2}, \quad a_{h,\text{des}} = - K_p (h_{\text{rel}} - h_{\text{rel},\text{nom}}) - K_d \dot{z}_{\text{base}}$$ This eliminates height tension whether the robot is on the platform, driving off drop ledges, or negotiating sinusoidal hills.
  • Gravity Support Prior (Fixing Free-Fall Minimization): Under torque minimization ($\min \|\boldsymbol{\tau}\|^2$), the QP previously favored $F_z \to 0$ (free-fall collapse under gravity). A quadratic prior: $$w_{f_z} \|F_z - \frac{1}{2} m_{\text{tot}} g\|^2$$ guarantees the optimizer maintains positive ground normal force ($\approx 9.5\,\text{N}$ per wheel).
  • Stiff Hip Roll Posture Regularization (Fixing Lateral Toppling): The hip roll joints (joints 1 & 5) have a mechanical hard stop at $\pm 0.60\,\text{rad}$ ($34.4^\circ$). Assigning high-stiffness posture tracking ($w_{\text{post\_roll}} = 35.0, k_p = 45.0, k_d = 8.0$) prevents the legs from splaying outward and kicking the chassis into a rollover.
  • Autonomous Position-Hold Lock: When $v_{\text{target}} = 0$, the controller locks $y_{\text{hold}}$ and commands $v_{\text{adj}} = -1.5(y - y_{\text{hold}})$, preventing forward drift off platform ledges.

9. Dual-Lane Sinusoidal Wavy Terrain Runway

To rigorously test 3D multi-contact active suspension, bilateral height decoupling, and disturbance rejection, a custom procedural terrain runway was developed in CAD/wavy_scene.xml.

Figure 3: Topographic cross-section of the dual-lane 180° out-of-phase sinusoidal runway. Left and right wheels drive at different elevations at different times.

Mathematical Formulation of Terrain Lanes

The terrain elevation is parameterized along the forward axis $y$:

$$z(x, y) = A \cdot \sin\left(\frac{2\pi y}{\lambda} + \phi(x)\right), \quad \phi(x) = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } x < x_{\text{mid}} \quad \text{(Right Wheel Lane)} \\ \pi & \text{if } x \ge x_{\text{mid}} \quad \text{(Left Wheel Lane)} \end{cases}$$

With $A = 0.035\,\text{m}$ ($3.5\,\text{cm}$ amplitude) and $\lambda = 1.0\,\text{m}$ wavelength:

  • When the right wheel is at a wave crest ($z_R = +3.5\,\text{cm}$), the left wheel is at a wave trough ($z_L = -3.5\,\text{cm}$).
  • The peak elevation differential between bilateral wheel hubs is $\Delta z = 7.0\,\text{cm}$, requiring the active suspension and hip joints to articulate independently.

Active Leveling & Normal Force Equalization

To prevent the chassis from rolling violently with the terrain slopes, the WBC QP incorporates:

  1. Active Roll Leveling: $a_{\text{roll},\text{des}} = - K_{p,\text{roll}} \psi_{\text{base}} - K_{d,\text{roll}} \dot{\psi}_{\text{base}}$, driving base roll to zero.
  2. Normal Force Equalization: Soft quadratic objective penalty $w_{\text{fb}} (F_{z,R} - F_{z,L})^2$ forcing the optimizer to distribute contact traction equally between the higher and lower wheel.

10. Acrobatic 360° Backflip Stunt Verification with WBC

The acrobatic stunt capability was verified using an autonomous multi-phase state machine triggered interactively via the B key:

  1. Crouch ($t = 0.00\,\text{s}$ to $0.20\,\text{s}$): Deep hip and knee compression ($q_k \to -0.60\,\text{rad}$) pre-loading the virtual suspension springs.
  2. Whip ($t = 0.20\,\text{s}$ to $0.216\,\text{s}$): Explosive torque pulse ($\tau_{\text{hip}} = +14.0\,\text{Nm}$) whipping the torso backward into rotational acceleration ($\dot{\theta} \approx 27.3\,\text{rad/s}$).
  3. Pop & Liftoff ($t = 0.216\,\text{s}$ to $0.25\,\text{s}$): Full knee extension thrusting the center of mass vertically into the air ($v_z = 6.78\,\text{m/s}$).
  4. Tucked Flight ($t = 0.25\,\text{s}$ to $0.57\,\text{s}$): Knees tuck tightly, passing through inverted apex at $z = 0.548\,\text{m}$ ($\text{Up-Z} = -0.9837$) with $181.7^\circ$ rotation.
  5. Untuck & Touchdown ($t = 0.57\,\text{s}$): Legs extend symmetrically to meet the platform at $344.7^\circ$ rotation.
  6. WBC Re-Settling ($t > 0.57\,\text{s}$): Whole-Body QP catches the touchdown impulse, dissipating vertical energy and restoring rock-steady balance ($\text{Up-Z} = 1.0000$).

Sim-to-Real Deployment Architecture with RL

Hierarchical RL + WBC Pipeline:

The modular structure established in this repository is directly architected for future sim-to-real reinforcement learning deployment:

  1. High-Level RL Planner ($50\,\text{Hz}$): An actor-critic policy network trained in MuJoCo / IsaacGym processes terrain elevation map inputs, IMU orientation, and joystick commands, outputting high-level motion references: $$\mathbf{a}_{\text{RL}} = [v_{\text{target}},\, \theta_{\text{target}},\, \psi_{\text{target}},\, \Delta h_{\text{target}}]^T$$
  2. Low-Level WBC QP ($1.0\,\text{kHz}$): The Whole-Body QP controller runs at $1\,\text{kHz}$ directly on the robot's onboard microcontroller or compute board, taking the RL references and computing motor torques while strictly enforcing motor torque saturation, joint limits, and friction cone boundaries.

In this configuration, the WBC acts as an exact physical safety filter, shielding the robot hardware from out-of-distribution neural policy outputs while providing agile terrain traversal.
