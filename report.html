The robot is an agile wheeled biped consisting of a floating base pelvis/torso and two bilateral 2-link articulated legs terminating in independent driven hub wheels. The mechanical CAD assembly (exported from OnShape into CAD/robot.xml and CAD/scene.xml) defines a 6-DOF actuated system resting on ground plane contacts.

2. CAD Asymmetric Assembly Calibration & Nominal Stance

Direct import from OnShape revealed that the right leg was mated at a different initial mechanical zero relative to the left leg. Without calibration, the right wheel dangled $3.1\,\text{cm}$ higher than the left wheel, causing the robot to violently roll and topple upon contact.

Exact Numerical Calibration Solved to Sub-Micron Precision: $$\Delta q_{\text{right\_hip\_calib}} = +0.258385\,\text{rad} \quad (+14.804^\circ)$$ $$\Delta q_{\text{right\_knee\_calib}} = +0.552626\,\text{rad} \quad (+31.663^\circ)$$ Applying these offsets yields a vertical wheel axle difference of $|z_{\text{wheel,L}} - z_{\text{wheel,R}}| < 0.65\,\mu\text{m}$, ensuring both wheels touch the ground plane simultaneously with zero rolling tilt.

Nominal Sagittal Stance Alignment

To balance without spending continuous motor torque fighting gravity, the robot's center of mass must align perfectly along the vertical line intersecting the wheel contact axle:

$$y_{\text{COM}}(q_h, q_k) - y_{\text{wheel}} = 0$$

By solving this constraint numerically against the complete multi-body tree, we discovered the unique nominal hip angle:

$$q_{h,\text{nominal}} = -0.2150\,\text{rad} \quad (-12.318^\circ), \quad q_{k,\text{nominal}} = 0.0\,\text{rad}$$

At this stance, $|y_{\text{COM}} - y_{\text{wheel}}| = 0.00004\,\text{m}$ ($0.04\,\text{mm}$), and the wheel tires contact the floor plane at exactly $z = 0.0012\,\text{m}$. This state is encoded as a native MuJoCo keyframe:

3. Nonlinear Dynamics & acados SQP-RTI Formulation

The coupled dynamics between forward translation $p$ along $+Y$, pitch rotation $\theta$ around lateral $+X$, and articulated leg kinematics $q_h, q_k$ are governed by the Euler-Lagrange equations of motion:

$$M(q)\ddot{q} + C(q, \dot{q})\dot{q} + G(q) = B u$$

For the 8-dimensional state vector $x = [p, v, \theta, \omega, q_h, \dot{q}_h, q_k, \dot{q}_k]^T$ and control inputs $u = [\tau_w, \tau_h, \tau_k]^T$:

$$\begin{aligned} M_{\text{tot}} &= M + m + \frac{I_w}{R^2} \\ \det M &= M_{\text{tot}}(I_y + M l^2) - (M l \cos\theta)^2 \\ f_v &= \frac{\tau_w}{R} + M l \omega^2 \sin\theta \\ f_\theta &= M g l \sin\theta - \tau_w - \tau_h \\ \dot{v} &= \frac{(I_y + M l^2) f_v - (M l \cos\theta) f_\theta}{\det M} \\ \dot{\omega} &= \frac{-(M l \cos\theta) f_v + M_{\text{tot}} f_\theta}{\det M} \\ \ddot{q}_h &= \frac{\tau_h - b_h \dot{q}_h - 0.1 g \sin(q_h)}{I_h} \\ \ddot{q}_k &= \frac{\tau_k - b_k \dot{q}_k - 0.1 g \sin(q_k)}{I_k} \end{aligned}$$

Optimal Control Problem (OCP) Multiple-Shooting Discretization

The tracking objective over horizon $T_f = 0.5\,\text{s}$ ($N = 25$ stages, control interval $\Delta t = 20\,\text{ms}$, rate $50\,\text{Hz}$) is formulated as:

$$\min_{x_{0:N}, u_{0:N-1}} \frac{1}{2} \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \left( \Vert{} y_k - y_{\text{ref}} \Vert{}_{W}^2 \right) + \frac{1}{2} \Vert{} x_N - x_{\text{ref},e} \Vert{}_{W_e}^2$$ $$\text{subject to:} \quad x_0 = \hat{x}_t, \quad x_{k+1} = \Phi_{\text{ERK4}}(x_k, u_k), \quad -\tau_{\max} \le u_k \le \tau_{\max}$$

State and control weight matrices:

$$W = \operatorname{diag}\left( [20.0,\, 5.0,\, 150.0,\, 25.0,\, 250.0,\, 15.0,\, 250.0,\, 15.0,\, 0.01,\, 0.005,\, 0.005] \right)$$ $$W_e = 2.5 \cdot W_{x,x}$$
SQP-RTI Computational Efficiency: Generated with CasADi symbolic graphs and compiled into native C via export_acados.py, the real-time iteration scheme executes a single Gauss-Newton quadratic program via HPIPM. In our C++ benchmark (bazel run //:mpc_sim), the average solve latency is $478.8\,\mu\text{s}$ ($0.48\,\text{ms}$), providing a $41\times$ margin within the $20\,\text{ms}$ control cycle.

4. Non-Minimum Phase Dynamics & Drift Elimination

A common failure mode in wheeled inverted pendulum control is backward runaway drift. Understanding why this occurs requires examining the system's non-minimum phase zero dynamics.

Figure 2: Cascaded control architecture. Position error commands an intentional lean angle θ_ref, trimmed by integral action.

Mathematical Derivation of Cascaded Drift Compensation

In MuJoCo's coordinate system, rotation by $\theta > 0$ around lateral $+X$ tilts the top of the torso backward into $-Y$. Positive wheel motor torque ($\tau_w > 0$) accelerates the wheel contact point forward into $+Y$.

If a direct position term is added to torque ($\tau_w = -K_p p$), when the robot drifts backward ($p < 0$), the controller commands a positive wheel torque $\tau_w > 0$. Accelerating the base forward induces an inertial reaction that pitches the torso backward. The inner pitch stabilizer must then immediately reverse torque to avoid falling, causing perpetual backward runaway.

The Cascaded Solution: To move forward in $+Y$, an inverted pendulum must first tilt forward ($\theta_{\text{ref}} < 0$). $$\begin{aligned} p_{\text{err}} &= p - p_{\text{target}} \\ p_{\text{int}}(t) &= \operatorname{clamp}\left( \int_0^t p_{\text{err}}(\tau) d\tau, \, -0.5, \, 0.5 \right) \\ \theta_{\text{ref}} &= \operatorname{clamp}\left( K_{p,p} \cdot p_{\text{err}} + K_{d,p} \cdot v + K_{i,p} \cdot p_{\text{int}}, \, -0.10, \, 0.10 \right) \\ \tau_w &= -\left( K_{p,\theta}(\theta - \theta_{\text{ref}}) + K_{d,\theta}\omega \right) \end{aligned}$$ Tuning parameters: $K_{p,p} = 0.35$, $K_{d,p} = 0.30$, $K_{i,p} = 0.05$, $K_{p,\theta} = 50.0$, $K_{d,\theta} = 7.0$.

5. Symmetrical Vertical Crouch Control & Height Kinematics

The robot's 4 active leg joints allow full real-time regulation of vertical height while simultaneously balancing on its wheels.

Figure 3: Kinematic progression from Standing Tall (17.2 cm) through Nominal (15.9 cm) to Crouched (14.0 cm).

Coordinated Hip Equilibrium Compensation

When the knees flex or extend, the horizontal position of the robot's center of mass shifts. To maintain pure vertical motion without inducing pitch torque, the hip angle $q_h$ must compensate synchronously:

$$q_h(q_k) = q_{h,\text{nominal}} + 0.057 \cdot q_k^2$$

This kinematic coupling keeps the center of mass within $\mathbf{0.0001\,\text{m}}$ ($0.1\,\text{mm}$) of the wheel axle throughout the entire stroke.

The Right Leg Mirroring Inversion Bug & Resolution

Initial crouching tests exhibited severe sideways rolling tilt. Analysis of the CAD link frame orientations showed that:

  • In the left leg, joint angle $+q_k$ rotates the tibia in one direction around local $+Z$.
  • In the right leg, the coordinate frame is mirrored: applying $+q_k$ produces the opposite physical leg motion!
Exact Mirrored Posture Law: $$\begin{aligned} q_{\text{hip,L}} &= q_{h,\text{cmd}} \\ q_{\text{knee,L}} &= q_{k,\text{cmd}} \\ q_{\text{hip,R}} &= -q_{h,\text{cmd}} + q_{\text{right\_hip\_calib}} \\ q_{\text{knee,R}} &= \mathbf{-q_{k,\text{cmd}}} + q_{\text{right\_knee\_calib}} \end{aligned}$$ With the corrected negative sign on $q_{k,\text{cmd}}$, the roll tilt error during dynamic crouching is reduced to $< 0.002^\circ$ and the left/right wheel axle elevation difference is constrained to $< 0.7\,\mu\text{m}$.

CAD Ground Clearance Constraint

The wheel radius is $R = 40\,\text{mm}$. The tibia lower strut (which houses the hub and timing belt) has a radial profile of $\approx 32\,\text{mm}$. The ground clearance between the strut and the floor is therefore:

$$c_{\text{clearance}} = R - r_{\text{tibia}} \approx 8\,\text{mm}$$

Rotating the tibia beyond $q_k = -15^\circ$ causes the collision mesh of the tibia strut to scrape against the floor plane, lifting the wheel off the ground. Knee angle is therefore constrained to $q_k \in [-14^\circ, +12^\circ]$, delivering $3.2\,\text{cm}$ of smooth vertical travel without contact risk.

6. Interactive Driving & Inward Roll Banking Lean

Omnidirectional mobility and trajectory tracking are implemented using 3D coordinate-invariant attitude tracking and dynamic inward roll banking.

3D Invariant Orientation Tracking

In a 3D simulation with turning, pitch and roll must not be computed relative to fixed world axes. Using the base rotation matrix $R = [x_{\text{lat}},\, y_{\text{fwd}},\, z_{\text{up}}]$:

$$\theta = \arcsin\left(\operatorname{clamp}(y_{\text{fwd}}[2], -1, 1)\right), \quad \phi = \arcsin\left(\operatorname{clamp}(x_{\text{lat}}[2], -1, 1)\right)$$ $$v_{\text{fwd}} = \vec{v}_{\text{world}} \cdot \vec{y}_{\text{fwd}}$$

This ensures that regardless of whether the robot faces $+Y$, $-X$, or any 360-degree heading, the longitudinal balance controller operates strictly along the body's heading axis with zero cross-axis gyroscopic cross-talk.

Inward Roll Banking Lean (Biped Banking)

When executing a turn at forward velocity $v$ with yaw rate $\dot{\psi}$, the robot experiences a lateral centrifugal acceleration $a_c = v \dot{\psi}$ acting on its center of mass. Left uncompensated, this centrifugal moment causes the wheeled biped to tip outward and roll over.

Because both legs are independently actuated, the robot generates an active inward banking lean by differentially altering the virtual leg lengths:

$$\Delta q_{k,\text{bank}} = -k_{\text{bank}} \cdot \dot{\psi}_{\text{target}}$$ $$\begin{aligned} q_{k,L} &= q_{k,\text{cmd}} + \Delta q_{k,\text{bank}} \\ q_{k,R} &= q_{k,\text{cmd}} - \Delta q_{k,\text{bank}} \\ q_{h,L} &= q_{h,\text{nominal}} + 0.057 \cdot q_{k,L}^2 \\ q_{h,R} &= q_{h,\text{nominal}} + 0.057 \cdot q_{k,R}^2 \end{aligned}$$

When steering left ($\dot{\psi} > 0$), the left leg squats slightly while the right leg extends, tilting the pelvis into the turn (up to $\sim 3^\circ-5^\circ$ roll banking). Differential wheel torque ($\Delta \tau_w = -K_{\text{yaw}} \Delta \omega_{\text{yaw}}$) provides precise heading tracking.

7. Platform Drop Scene & Active Suspension Control

To validate dynamic shock absorption and compliance, the simulation scene starts with the robot perched on a raised platform with an $8\,\text{cm}$ ledge drop:

Platform Geometry:
  • Top Surface Elevation: $z = 0.08\,\text{m}$ ($8\,\text{cm}$ above ground plane).
  • Platform Extents: From $y = -0.80\,\text{m}$ to $y = +0.40\,\text{m}$ (width $1.20\,\text{m}$).
  • Initial Spawn / Home Keyframe: Robot base at $z = 0.0812\,\text{m}$, resting on top of the platform.
  • Ledge Drop: At $y = +0.40\,\text{m}$, the platform terminates abruptly, requiring the robot to drive off the edge, free-fall $8\,\text{cm}$, and land on the floor plane at $z = 0.00\,\text{m}$.

Active Suspension Virtual Impedance

A rigid position-controlled leg would bounce violently or transmit a catastrophic shock impulse on landing ($v_{\text{impact}} \approx \sqrt{2gh} \approx 1.25\,\text{m/s}$, impact energy $E \approx 1.66\,\text{J}$).

The active suspension implements variable virtual impedance control with impact shock dissipation:

$$\tau_{\text{knee}} = -K_{p,\text{susp}} (q_k - q_{k,\text{target}}) - K_{d,\text{susp}}(\dot{q}_k) \dot{q}_k$$ $$K_{d,\text{susp}}(\dot{q}_k) = \begin{cases} 8.5\,\text{N}\cdot\text{s/rad} & \text{if } |\dot{q}_k| > 1.2\,\text{rad/s} \quad \text{(Touchdown impact phase)} \\ 4.0\,\text{N}\cdot\text{s/rad} & \text{otherwise} \quad \text{(Nominal compliance)} \end{cases}$$

Airborne Anti-Windup Protection

During the $8\,\text{cm}$ drop ($z \in [0.015,\, 0.075]\,\text{m}$ with $\dot{z} < -0.2\,\text{m/s}$), normal ground contact forces vanish. The controller detects this flight phase, freezes the longitudinal position integrator ($p_{\text{int}} = 0.0$), and matches $p_{\text{target}} = p$, preventing integrator windup. Upon wheel touchdown, the balancer catches pitch smoothly within a single cycle.
